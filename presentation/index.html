SLO & SLA Framework

A simple, standard way to define, measure and promise service reliability

Based on the Google SRE Workbook — Implementing SLOs

Goal of this session: agree on one simple way for every team to define and manage reliability, and get decisions on a few open points at the end.

Why this framework

Alignment

Aligns how services and products are delivered across teams.

Data-driven decisions

Gives a shared, measurable basis for balancing reliability and new features.

Ready to scale

One repeatable method as services and the dependencies between teams grow.

Framed as what the framework enables, not as a statement about how teams work today.

Four words

SLI — what we measure: good events ÷ total events
"VM creates that were running within 5 min ÷ all VM creates"
SLO — the internal target
"99% over a rolling 4 weeks"
Error budget — the failure we're allowed = 100% − SLO
"1% of creates may fail or be slow"
SLA — the promise to consumers, looser than the SLO
"97% per month, P1 response 15 min"
SLI is a percentage, always the same shape, so dashboards and alerts are reusable. SLA is always looser than SLO, so we react before we break a promise. 100% is the wrong target.

The error budget decides

SLO 99.5% over 4 weeks → budget = 3 h 22 min of downtime

Storage blip 40 min · bad upgrade 1 h · node failure 10 min → 55% used

Budget left

Ship features, upgrade, experiment. Unused budget is wasted.

Budget gone

Pause risky changes, fix reliability until it recovers.

This is the core idea: data, not opinion, decides between features and reliability. The rule is written down in an error budget policy that the team agrees to upfront.

Steps 2–6 repeat · first SLO doesn't have to be perfect — measure, agree, improve

1: list users and 3-5 key tasks. 2: max 5 SLIs from a ready-made menu. 3: measure last 4 weeks, round down. 4: three sign-offs + error budget policy. 5: dashboard and burn-rate alerts. 6: monthly then quarterly review. 7: SLA only after about a quarter of meeting the SLO.

SLO document

Error budget policy

Template

Agreed in advance: what the team does when the allowed failure is used up.

KubeVirt platform: budget gone → hub and KV cluster upgrades freeze; tenants can still create and scale clusters.

SLA

1 page

The promise to tenants. Looser than the SLO, published after about a quarter of meeting it.

KubeVirt platform: you choose your worker upgrade window; workers never fall more than two minor versions behind.

Templates and the full KubeVirt platform example are in the repo · stored in git next to the service code

Deliberately light: three short documents, templates based on Google's published examples. Examples come from the HCP + KubeVirt platform documents in examples/hcp-kubevirt-platform. The SLA example is a non-numeric commitment because its targets wait for real data.

Shows the flow: measure, round down to SLO, SLA looser still. The dependency check turned an unrealistic target into a visible investment decision instead of a broken promise.

A service is "done" when…

  • SLOs are approved as right for the users
  • The people running it agree they're achievable without heroics
  • Everyone commits to using the error budget to make decisions
  • There's a review process to keep improving them
The four conditions from the SRE Workbook. Without them, an SLO is just another report.
